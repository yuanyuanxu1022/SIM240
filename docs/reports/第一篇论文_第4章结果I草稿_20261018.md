## IV. RESULTS I: THE RESOLVED COEFFICIENTS

### EN

#### A. What the solved flow gives

We report first the resolved coefficients themselves, before any comparison with a closure. Two quantities are needed, $K_\parallel$ (transport along the grooves, driven along $y$) and $K_\perp$ (transport across the grooves, driven along $x$), obtained from Eq. (4) at steady state for the eighteen geometries of TABLE I, each in two principal directions (thirty-six runs). Both are quoted as $K$ in m³, consistent with the per-unit-width definition of Sec. II G. The volume-averaged velocity is $\bar u=V^{-1}\int_V u\,dV=J/H_{\rm ref}$, so conversion to the three-dimensional permeability used in porous-media and heat-pipe practice requires division by the equivalent-volume height, $K^{[\mathrm{m^2}]}=K^{[\mathrm{m^3}]}/H_{\rm ref}$, $H_{\rm ref}=h+\varphi d$ with $\varphi=w/P$; a period-averaged apparent velocity would require a separate declaration and is not used here.

**FIG. 4** shows the result. Panel (a) gives $K_\parallel$ and $K_\perp$ against groove depth $d$ for each of the four gaps investigated; panel (b) gives their ratio.

#### B. The two components depend on different variables

The most useful observation from FIG. 4 is not the magnitude of either coefficient but the distinct way each responds to the geometry.

**The across-groove coefficient is dominated by the gap.** At fixed gap, $K_\perp$ varies only weakly with $d$ over the sampled depths (for $h=25$ nm the sampled depths are only $d=25$, $50$ and $100$ nm): 1.037× at $h=25$ nm, 1.034× at 50 nm, 1.021× at 75 nm and 1.009× at 125 nm — the thinnest gap retains the largest (but still small) depth sensitivity. Within the sampled range, what controls $K_\perp$ is the gap $h$, and it does so strongly: $K_\perp$ rises by a factor of $87$ from $2.14\times10^{-24}$ m³ at $h=25$ nm to $1.86\times10^{-22}$ m³ at $h=125$ nm.

**The along-groove coefficient depends on both.** $K_\parallel$ rises with $d$ at fixed $h$, and the rise is itself gap-dependent: from $d=25$ to $120$ nm at $h=50$ nm it grows by a factor of $3.55$, at $h=75$ nm by $2.13$, and at $h=125$ nm by only $1.39$. So the groove adds transport capacity along its axis chiefly when the gap above it is thin.

This asymmetry admits a physical reading, which we state as a candidate interpretation rather than an established mechanism, because it organises what follows. One candidate is that **transport across the grooves must pass the groove mouth**, where the flow contracts into the groove on one side and expands out of it on the other; the resistance of such a contraction–expansion pair would belong to the mouth rather than to the volume behind it, so that a deeper groove would not widen the mouth. **Transport along the grooves runs inside the groove**, so its conductance would be set by the groove cross-section and would grow with $d$. The resolved coefficients reported above are spatially integrated quantities: they are consistent with this reading but cannot localise where the resistance lies, and they do not exclude competing explanations such as wall shear at the side walls or redistribution of the velocity field near the steps.

#### C. The anisotropy depends on both the gap and the depth, with the depth contribution growing at thin gaps

Panel (b) renders the same statement as a ratio. Across the eighteen geometries the anisotropy spans $A=1.090$ to $15.45$ ($5$ nm production-grid maximum), a factor of fourteen. At fixed $d=100$ nm, $A$ falls from $15.45$ at $h=25$ nm to $2.210$ at $h=75$ nm and $1.432$ at $h=125$ nm. The depth is not ineffective, however: at fixed $h=125$ nm, $A$ rises from $1.090$ to $1.503$ as $d$ goes from 25 to 120 nm ($1.4\times$), while at fixed $h=25$ nm it rises from $2.282$ to $15.45$ as $d$ goes from 25 to 100 nm ($6.8\times$; $d=75$ and $d=120$ nm were not computed at this gap). Within the sampled range, gap and depth jointly set the anisotropy, and the depth contribution strengthens as the gap thins.

Two limits bound the family of curves, and both are physically meaningful.

- **$A\to1$ as $d\to0$.** With no groove the cross-section is a plane channel and the two directions are equivalent. The data approach this limit from above as $d$ becomes small at large gap: $A=1.090$ at $(h,d)=(125,25)$ nm.
- **$A$ increases steadily as the gap thins within the sampled range.** The trend in panel (b) steepens as $h$ decreases within the sampled range, and the smallest gap computed, $h=25$ nm with $d=100$ nm, already gives $A=15.45$ on the $5$ nm production grid ($16.10$ at $\Delta x=2.5$ nm, Sec. III G2). We did not compute below 25 nm; whether $A$ diverges as $h\to0$ is not established and no extrapolation is made.

The practical consequence is the one that matters for process design: **within the sampled range, the directionality of transport in an imprint groove is set jointly by the gap and the groove depth, with the depth contribution strengthening as the gap thins.** A designer who wants to reduce directionality — to make the resist flow more evenly in both in-plane directions — can act on either parameter: shallower grooves or a wider gap both lower $A$. Which is preferable is a process constraint, not a result of these calculations. Within the sampled range the depth contribution is largest at thin gaps; a quantitative ranking of the two would require a defined measure of relative parameter change, which this sampled grid does not support.

#### D. Equivalent-volume efficiency

A quantity that makes the second point quantitative is the efficiency with which a given volume of groove contributes to transport. Let $H_{\rm ref}=h+\varphi d$ be the volume-equivalent height, Eq. (7), with $\varphi=w/P=0.5$. A plane channel of that height would have conductance $H_{\rm ref}^3/12$. The ratio

$$
\eta_{\alpha}=\frac{K_{\alpha}^{[\mathrm{m^3}]}}{H_{\rm ref}^{3}/12},\qquad \alpha=\parallel,\perp
\tag{10}
$$

measures how much of the transport capacity implied by the volume is actually delivered. These are discrete numerical results for the conductance relative to an equal-volume plane channel; $\eta$ can exceed $1$ (a conductance ratio above the reference) and must not be read as a volume fraction.

Over the eighteen sampled geometries, $\eta_\parallel$ ranges from $0.531$ to $1.147$ — at $h=25$ nm with $d=25$ and $50$ nm the grooved conductance exceeds that of the equal-volume plane channel ($1.112$ and $1.147$; at $d=100$ nm it falls back to $0.976$) — while $\eta_\perp$ collapses from $0.852$ at $(h,d)=(125,25)$ nm down to $0.063$ at $(25,100)$ nm, a factor of $13.5$. At the most extreme sampled point, the cross-groove conductance is only about $6\%$ of the equal-volume plane-channel reference.

At fixed $d$, $\eta_\parallel$ varies with $h$ by up to a factor of $1.6$ once the $h=25$ nm row is included, so it is not $h$-independent; the gap sensitivity is nevertheless stronger for $\eta_\perp$. At $d=100$ nm, for example, $\eta_\parallel$ varies by a factor of $1.63$ over the sampled gaps while $\eta_\perp$ varies by $6.61$; at no single depth does $\eta_\perp$ vary by more than a factor of $6.7$ over the sampled gaps. This is the same asymmetry as in Sec. IV B, expressed as a volume efficiency rather than a coefficient.

#### E. Summary of this section

Three statements carry forward to the comparison with the closure.

1. $K_\perp$ is dominated by $h$ and varies only weakly with $d$ within the sampled range (up to $1.037\times$ at $h=25$ nm, under $1.04\times$ elsewhere).
2. $K_\parallel$ depends on both, and the contribution of groove depth is largest when the gap is thin.
3. Consequently the anisotropy $A$ depends on both the gap and the depth, and the depth contribution strengthens at thin gaps: at $d=100$ nm it changes by a factor of $11$ as $h$ goes from 25 to 125 nm, whereas over the sampled depth range it changes by $1.4\times$ at $h=125$ nm ($d$ from 25 to 120 nm) and by $6.8\times$ at $h=25$ nm ($d$ from 25 to 100 nm).

These are properties of the resolved flow and are established independently of any closure. Section V asks what a one-dimensional closure predicts for the same geometries, and why the two differ.

## 中文

### A. 解出来的系数

在与任何闭合比较之前，先报告解出来的系数本身。需要两个量：$K_\parallel$（沿槽输运，沿 $y$ 驱动）与 $K_\perp$（跨槽输运，沿 $x$ 驱动），由式 (4) 在稳态下对 TABLE I 的**十八种几何、各两个主方向（共 36 个工况）**求得。两者均以 m³ 给出，与 Sec. II G 的单位宽度定义一致。体积平均速度为 $\bar u=V^{-1}\int_V u\,dV=J/H_{\rm ref}$，故换算成多孔介质与热管惯用的三维渗透率应除以等体积高度：$K^{[\mathrm{m^2}]}=K^{[\mathrm{m^3}]}/H_{\rm ref}$，$H_{\rm ref}=h+\varphi d$，$\varphi=w/P$；若另用周期平均表观速度须单独声明，本文不采用。

**FIG. 4** 给出结果。面板 (a) 是四个间隙下 $K_\parallel$、$K_\perp$ 随槽深 $d$ 的变化；面板 (b) 是两者之比。

### B. 两个分量由不同的变量控制

FIG. 4 最有用的观察不是两个系数的大小，而是它们对几何的**响应方式不同**。

**跨槽系数以间隙为主导。** 固定间隙时 $K_\perp$ 在所采样深度上随 $d$ 变化很弱（$h=25$ nm 档仅算了 $d=25$、$50$、$100$ nm）：$h=25$ nm 时为 1.037 倍，50 nm 时 1.034 倍，75 nm 时 1.021 倍，125 nm 时 1.009 倍——最薄间隙保留了最大（但仍很小）的深度敏感性。**在所采样范围内，控制 $K_\perp$ 的主要是间隙 $h$，且影响很强**：$K_\perp$ 从 $h=25$ nm 的 $2.14\times10^{-24}$ m³ 增至 $h=125$ nm 的 $1.86\times10^{-22}$ m³，共 87 倍。

**沿槽系数由两者共同决定。** 固定 $h$ 时 $K_\parallel$ 随 $d$ 上升，而上升幅度本身又依赖间隙：$d$ 从 25 到 120 nm，$h=50$ nm 时增至 3.55 倍，$h=75$ nm 时 2.13 倍，$h=125$ nm 时仅 1.39 倍。**槽对沿槽输运能力的贡献，主要发生在它上方的间隙很薄的时候。**

这种不对称容许一种物理解读，但应作为**候选解释**而非已确立的机制陈述，因为它组织了后面的讨论。一种候选是：**跨槽输运必须经过槽口**——流体在一侧收缩进入槽内、另一侧扩张流出，这对收缩–扩张的阻力**可能**属于槽口而非槽口之后的容积，槽再深也不会让槽口变宽。**沿槽输运则在槽内进行**，其导度**可能**由槽的截面积决定，因而随 $d$ 增长。前文报告的已分辨系数是**空间积分量**：它们与这一解读相容，但不能定位阻力所在，也不能排除侧壁剪切或台阶附近速度场重分布等竞争性解释。

### C. 各向异性由间隙与槽深共同决定，槽深的贡献随间隙变薄而增强

面板 (b) 把同一事实画成比值。十八种几何中 $A$ 从 1.090 到 15.45（5 nm 生产网格最大值），跨 14 倍。固定 $d=100$ nm，$A$ 从 $h=25$ nm 的 15.45 降到 $h=75$ nm 的 2.210、再到 $h=125$ nm 的 1.432。但深度并非无效：固定 $h=125$ nm，$d$ 从 25 到 120 nm 时 $A$ 从 1.090 升到 1.503（1.4 倍）；而固定 $h=25$ nm，$d$ 从 25 到 100 nm 时 $A$ 从 2.282 升到 15.45（**6.8 倍**；该间隙下 $d=75$ 与 $d=120$ nm 未计算）。在所采样范围内，间隙与槽深共同决定各向异性，且槽深的影响随间隙变薄而增强。

两个极限界定了这一族曲线，且都有物理意义：

- **$d\to0$ 时 $A\to1$。** 没有槽，截面就是平行平板，两个方向等价。数据从上方趋近该极限——大间隙小槽深处 $A=1.090$（$h=125$、$d=25$ nm）。
- **固定 $d$、$h\to0$ 时 $A$ 在所采样范围内持续增大。** 面板 (b) 中 $h$ 越小趋势越陡；已算的最小间隙 $h=25$ nm 配 $d=100$ nm 时 $A$ 在 5 nm 生产网格上已达 15.45（$\Delta x=2.5$ nm 时为 16.10，见 Sec. III G2）。**25 nm 以下未计算；$h\to0$ 时 $A$ 是否发散未证实，此处不作外推。**

对工艺设计而言，最重要的推论是：**在所采样范围内，压印沟槽中输运的方向性由间隙与槽深共同决定，槽深的影响随间隙变薄而增强。** 若设计者想降低方向性——让胶在面内两个方向流得更均匀——**槽浅一些或间隙大一些都能降低 $A$，两者都可用**。选哪一个取决于工艺约束，而不是本计算给出的结论。在所采样范围内，槽深的影响在薄间隙时最大；要对两者作定量排序，需先定义相对参数变化的度量，而本文的采样网格不足以支撑这一点。

### D. 等体积效率

把第二点定量化的量，是"给定的槽容积有多少真正转化为输运能力"。设 $H_{\rm ref}=h+\varphi d$ 为等体积高度（式 7），$\varphi=w/P=0.5$；同高度的平行平板导度为 $H_{\rm ref}^3/12$。比值

$$
\eta_{\alpha}=\frac{K_{\alpha}^{[\mathrm{m^3}]}}{H_{\rm ref}^{3}/12},\qquad \alpha=\parallel,\perp
\tag{10}
$$

即衡量"由容积所隐含的输运能力有多大比例真正兑现"。这些是**离散数值结果相对等体积平板的导度比**；$\eta$ 可以大于 1（导度超过参照），**不能解读为体积分数**。

在所采样的十八种几何上，$\eta_\parallel$ 范围为 **0.531–1.147**——$h=25$ nm 配 $d=25$、$50$ nm 两点时沟槽导度超过了等体积平板（1.112 与 1.147；$d=100$ nm 时回落到 0.976）——而 $\eta_\perp$ 从 $(h,d)=(125,25)$ nm 的 0.852 塌陷到 $(25,100)$ nm 的 **0.063**，相差 **13.5 倍**。在最极端的采样点处，跨槽导度仅为等体积平板参照的约 **6%**。

### E. 本章小结

三条结论将带入与闭合的比较：

1. $K_\perp$ 以 $h$ 为主导，所采样范围内对 $d$ 变化很弱（$h=25$ nm 时最大 1.037 倍，其余 <1.04 倍）。
2. $K_\parallel$ 由两者共同决定，且槽深的贡献在间隙薄时最大。
3. 因此各向异性 $A$ 由间隙与槽深共同决定，槽深贡献随间隙变薄而增强：$d=100$ nm 时 $h$ 从 25 到 125 nm 使 $A$ 变化 11 倍，而在所采样深度范围内，$h=125$ nm 时变化 1.4 倍（$d$ 从 25 到 120 nm）、$h=25$ nm 时变化 6.8 倍（$d$ 从 25 到 100 nm）。

这些都是**解出来的流动本身**的性质，其成立与任何闭合无关。第 5 章将问：同样这些几何上，一维闭合会预测出什么，以及两者为何不同。

## 写作说明

### 一、本章的定位

**这一章刻意不做对比。** 第 5 章才谈闭合误差。分开的好处：

1. 第 4 章的数字是"真实值"，可以独立成立、独立被引用；
2. 第 5 章的误差表有了干净基准，不必在同一个图里混两类信息；
3. 审稿人若质疑闭合，不会连带质疑 K 本身。

### 二、本章的主线：不是"K 有多大"，而是"两个分量由不同变量控制"

这是数据自己长出来的结论（$K_\perp$ 在所采样范围内随 $d$ 变化很弱、$K_\parallel$ 则随两者变化），不是我预设的框架。它的用处在于：

- 它给出了"该调什么参数"的经验指引；
- 它为第 5 章修正式的变量选择提供了**动机**。

**但不能说它"证明了"单变量 $d/w$ 修正的充分性**：CORE_CLAIMS 第 33 行明确"不能据一变量拟合断言两方向误差只由 $d/w$ 决定"；观测到的分量响应是空间积分量的性质，不等于已识别出误差的物理来源。两者须分别表述。

### 三、关于「$\eta$ 与死空间」的措辞（已撤回）

$\eta_\perp$ 是**相对等体积平板的导度比**，不是体积分数，因此 $1-\eta$ 不能解读为"死空间比例"或"阻力位置"。此前"82% / 87% 容积是死空间"的说法已撤回。若需表达跨槽导度很弱，只写导度比本身（如 $\eta_\perp=0.063$，即仅为参照的 6%），不做容积占比或阻力空间分布的推断。
