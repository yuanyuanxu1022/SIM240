                Abstract
                                                           er
                Micromixers are widely used chemical processing devices that are applied
                for both mixing and reacting flows. In current research, the laminar reacting
                                                 pe
                multi-component flows are considered [1, 2, 3]. Despite the laminar streaming
                regime (e.g. Re = 186), there exist flow micro-structures. For this setup,
                accurate predictions of that structures are possible with a direct numerical
                simulation on a fine mesh resolving the Batchelor microscales.
                    Utilizing the open-source Lattice Boltzmann Methods (LBM) framework
                                       ot

                OpenLB (www.openlb.net, [4, 5]) a precise benchmark simulation of the re-
                acting micromixer was set up and validated based on previous work by Bothe
                et al. [1, 2, 3]. In this context, a Schmidt number based stabilization method
                for reactive advection-diffusion equations is proposed. A convergence study
                             tn



                is performed which is also a novelty. All computations were performed on
                the HPC cluster HoreKa using up to 160 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
                Keywords: benchmark simulation, micromixer, LBM, DNS, reaction,
                rin




                advection-diffusion equation

                    Estimation of the contact area between mixing species is a decisive factor
                                               pe
                in the design of a micromixer geometry. Due to computational power limi-
                tations, the accurate tracking of the fluid structures that increase this area
                has been impossible until recently. The design process of the micromixers
                was based on the estimation of the geometrical Dean number, which serves
                as a characteristic number for the secondary flow. The mixing efficiency was
                                     ot

                then evaluated as a result of this estimation. Nowadays, computational fluid
                dynamics (CFD) allows precise calculation of the flow behavior. The most
                reliable results are achieved by direct numerical simulation (DNS), where
                the cell size is less or equal to the finest length scale of the investigated flow
                          tn



                structures. Although the operation state of the regarded micromixer is lami-
                nar, the length scale is bounded from below by the Kolmogorow length scale,
                at which the kinetic energy of the vortex is dissipated into the thermal en-
                ergy due to the friction of streaming layers [11]. The species concentrations
                rin




                transported by the micro-flow form smaller structures within the vortex of
                the carrier fluid. Therefore, in order to simulate the mixing process in the
                micromixer accurately, a finer cell size is required than given by the hydrody-
                namic length scale. Specifically, the simulation needs to resolve the Batchelor
     ep

                                     ot
