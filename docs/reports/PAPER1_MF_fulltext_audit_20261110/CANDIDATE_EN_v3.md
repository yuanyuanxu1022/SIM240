## Section 1.2: insert after the existing second paragraph

Semi-analytical studies of directional flow in rectangular grooves are available. Ng [Ng] used domain decomposition and eigenfunction matching to solve no-slip pressure-driven Stokes flow between two rectangular-grooved walls, examining pattern phase shifts and wall spacing. This differs from the present flat-wall/grooved-wall problem; the mid-plane of his symmetric configuration cannot replace the present no-slip plate. Moreover, his longitudinal effective slip length uses only the clear-layer flux between the wall envelopes, explicitly excluding cavity flux, so the slip length alone does not provide the present conductance integrated over all fluid.

The pressure-loss study of Mohammadi and Floryan [MF] uses a Fourier framework for the two wall shapes, permits a planar wall, and includes rectangular grooves in tests of geometry reduction. Rectangular shape alone therefore cannot exclude that study. Detailed parameter results mainly concern small-amplitude sinusoidal grooves and distinguish changes in mean wall position from geometry-induced flow modulation. The leading-mode approximation, the full numerical solution and the displayed parameter range must be distinguished. A matched comparison at the present rectangular geometry, low-inertia conditions, reference scales and flux definition has not been established here; these results are not adopted directly as a pointwise accuracy benchmark.

Stroock et al. [St] analyzed pressure-driven Stokes flow between a flat wall and a sinusoidally modulated wall of small amplitude, describing anisotropic effective boundary conditions and transverse flow induced by oblique grooves, with an experimental check. Directional response, wall-confinement effects and anisotropy-induced flow deflection are therefore not presented as new findings here. Our assessment remains limited to local-cubic-law discrepancies and their consequences for a specified rectangular family, supplemented by a gradient-structure diagnostic under a defined decomposition on a separate set. Differences in geometry or grouping alone do not establish priority.

## Section 2.2: clarify the existing H_ref and κ relation

Here, $H_{\rm ref}=V_f/A_{\rm plan}=h+d/2$ is the geometric mean aperture for equal groove and ridge widths, used to convert between mean velocity, depth-integrated flux and $\kappa$; it differs from an equivalent smooth-channel opening defined by matching hydraulic resistance, such as $EC_h$ in Mohammadi and Floryan [MF], Section 4.4.2.
