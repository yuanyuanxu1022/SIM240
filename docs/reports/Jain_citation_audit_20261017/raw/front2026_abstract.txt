Abstract
Transient filling in confined microgrooves involves interactions among viscous resistance, capillary effects, wall confinement, wetting behavior, and free-surface evolution. Direct observation of transient front morphology is therefore important for identifying localized redistribution that may not be evident from the final replicated structure alone. This study investigated confined microgroove filling using a commercial ultraviolet (UV)-curable acrylic resin as a low-nominal-capillary-number baseline and a high-viscosity silicone thermal paste as a comparison fluid for examining redistribution under higher nominal capillary-number conditions. A microscopic side-view imaging system was used to record transient front evolution during embossing, and corresponding two-dimensional ANSYS Polyflow simulations were conducted using an effective constant-viscosity Newtonian approximation. A nominal capillary number, \(\:{C}_{{a}_{n}}\), based on the effective reference viscosity, prescribed embossing-stage velocity, and surface tension was used to organize the investigated conditions. Under the investigated low-\(\:{C}_{{a}_{n}}\) UV-curable resin conditions, the liquid front remained comparatively smooth and nearly symmetric. In contrast, the higher-\(\:{C}_{{a}_{n}}\) JA447 comparison conditions showed progressively stronger lateral redistribution, including sidewall-localized accumulation and centerline suppression. The numerical model captured the general direction of this morphology evolution as \(\:{C}_{{a}_{n}}\) increased; however, the experimentally observed redistribution was more pronounced, particularly at the intermediate and higher JA447 conditions. The simulation therefore underestimated the magnitude of front-profile deformation. These results show that visualization and simplified free-surface simulation can be combined to examine the direction of transient morphology evolution during confined micro-embossing. However, the numerical results should be interpreted at the morphology-trend level rather than as quantitative reproduction of the experimental interface. The use of an effective Newtonian approximation, the two-dimensional model formulation, and incomplete archived numerical settings limit quantitative model validation. The reported \(\:{C}_{{a}_{n}}\) values should therefore be regarded as nominal comparison parameters under the present geometry and material conditions rather than as a universal rheological regime criterion.
