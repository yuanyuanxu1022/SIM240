## Abstract

The local cubic law is commonly used in reduced nanoimprint models, but its along- and across-groove conductances require separate assessment. Using the lattice Boltzmann method, we compute steady transport between a planar substrate and a periodically grooved wall, assuming a fixed gap, a fully filled Newtonian liquid domain and no-slip walls. The period is 240 nm, with equal groove and rib widths. The main dataset comprises 18 geometries and 36 principal-direction conductances on a 5 nm grid; four geometries also have 2.5 nm refinements to assess grid sensitivity.

Relative to the 5 nm discrete references, the local cubic law overestimates all main principal-direction results, with maximum relative discrepancies of +204.85% along and +54.19% across the grooves. At the reference geometry, anisotropy ratios from the original law and the discrete reference are 3.70 and 2.21, respectively. Reconstructing mean flux directions under the same linear tensor relation for forcing at 45° to the groove axis gives a difference of 9.20°. Retrospective evaluation of the fixed empirical correction (V2) reduces the maximum absolute discrepancies to 10.50% along and 18.71% across the grooves, although the improvement assessment at one thin-gap transverse point changes with reference grid. A comparison in both directions at one additional gap also reduces absolute relative discrepancy with V2 coefficients unchanged.

Theoretical analysis establishes local-cubic-law upper bounds on both continuous principal conductances in the stated periodic Stokes model, without determining discrepancy magnitudes or V2 accuracy. Quantitative results concern the sampled geometries and specified discrete references, without establishing continuous-domain or real-imprint accuracy guarantees. The findings support assessing both principal conductances, their ratio and reference sensitivity together in such reduced models.
