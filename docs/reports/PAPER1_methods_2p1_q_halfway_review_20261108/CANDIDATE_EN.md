# Section 2.1 fractional wall distance and halfway bounce-back candidate (not merged)

Replace only the paragraph beginning “Fractional wall distances are determined analytically…”; retain numbers and adjacent paragraphs.

The fractional wall distance $q$ is the distance from a fluid node to the wall intersection divided by the length of the corresponding fluid–solid node link. The boundary-audit set reported in the current manuscript contains 46 run records (36 main-map, 8 refinement and 2 viscosity checks, excluding the added $h=100$ nm cases), with recorded $q_{\min}=q_{\max}=0.5$ within tolerance $10^{-10}$. Thus, within that tolerance, wall intersections on the audited valid links lie at their midpoints. At $q=0.5$, the linear Bouzidi interpolation used for the stationary walls recovers the halfway bounce-back form [7,8]. These 46 boundary-audit records differ from the formula-comparison set of 19 geometries and 46 records. This geometric consistency alone does not establish zero boundary error or replace separate checks of link coverage, fallback handling and the geometry mask. The flat-wall convergence in Section 3 reflects the combined errors of discretization and flux extraction; it neither separates those contributions nor establishes accuracy at arbitrary fractional wall distances.
