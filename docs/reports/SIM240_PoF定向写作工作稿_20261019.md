## Title

Directional transport in confined rectangular grooves: errors and empirical correction of a local cubic-law closure

中文：受限矩形沟槽的方向性输运：局部立方律闭合的误差及经验修正。

若转MEE，标题可改为：Assessing anisotropic transport closures for fully filled nanoimprint templates with rectangular grooves。转刊重点是补清压印工程用途及范围，不改变数据或把固定间隙写成完整压印验证。

## Abstract

Reduced models of flow through patterned gaps require transport coefficients that retain the directional effects of unresolved grooves. We assess an arithmetic–harmonic local cubic-law closure using lattice Boltzmann calculations of steady, fully filled Newtonian flow between a planar wall and a periodically grooved wall. The groove period and width are 240 and 120 nm, respectively. Eighteen sampled geometries span gaps of 25–125 nm and groove depths of 25–120 nm. Relative to the discrete numerical references, the closure overestimates depth-integrated transport by 10.30–204.85% along the grooves and 8.11–54.19% across them. At a representative geometry, unequal errors in the principal coefficients produce a 9.20° discrepancy in the predicted flux direction under forcing at 45° to the grooves. A data-informed empirical correction reduces the maximum absolute residual to 10.5% over the 18 parallel-flow samples and 6.9% over the 15 transverse-flow samples with gaps of at least 50 nm. At a 25 nm gap and 100 nm groove depth, the transverse residual changes from −18.7% to −14.9% upon refinement from 5 to 2.5 nm. These two grids do not determine the convergence order, and no uncertainty bound is established for the full map. The correction is evaluated retrospectively rather than by independent prediction tests. The results quantify sampled directional closure discrepancies and delimit the evidence supporting the correction; they do not validate dynamic filling or transfer to real-resist behavior.

## I. INTRODUCTION（精简英文候选）

Flow in a confined patterned gap is relevant to nanoimprint lithography and to the wider modeling of liquid transport over textured surfaces. Resolving each groove provides a description of the local flow, whereas a reduced model replaces that geometry with effective transport coefficients. For a directional pattern, the two principal coefficients must be distinguished: their magnitude controls the response to a pressure gradient, and their ratio determines how the flux deviates from an oblique driving direction. A useful assessment of a reduced closure must therefore consider both transport magnitude and anisotropy. [draft:JC01]

Anisotropic transport modeling is already established in this context. Jain et al. developed a model for fluid flow beneath patterned templates in UV nanoimprint lithography [1]. Dewangan and Datta derived effective permeability tensors for confined grooved films using spectral analysis and domain perturbation and assessed representative topographies numerically [2]. These studies provide the relevant starting point. The question addressed here is the quantitative performance of a specified local cubic-law closure for a fully filled rectangular-groove geometry, with the reference definition, flow direction, and numerical resolution stated explicitly. [draft:JC02]

For alternating stripes, the arithmetic–harmonic closure treats each local depth through a parallel-plate cubic law and combines the responses according to the direction of transport. Under the same local-depth description and averaging convention, this construction represents the corresponding reduced Reynolds model. Agreement between two implementations of that construction consequently does not establish agreement with an explicit-geometry flow solution. The comparison is particularly relevant when the groove depth is comparable with its width and the residual gap is not uniformly small relative to the pattern dimensions. Side-wall shear and flow redistribution near the steps are plausible contributors to discrepancies, but a discrepancy in an effective coefficient alone does not separate those mechanisms. [draft:JC03]

We examine steady, fully filled flow of a Newtonian liquid with no-slip boundaries in ideal periodic rectangular grooves. Lattice Boltzmann calculations provide the depth-integrated transport coefficients for 18 geometries, each driven along and across the grooves. We first assess the sampled closure discrepancies and their effect on anisotropy and oblique flux direction. We then evaluate the adopted empirical correction together with its remaining dependence on the gap and its geometry-specific refinement evidence. The correction was selected with extension results available; its evaluation is retrospective, and neither a continuous-domain accuracy guarantee nor an independent final prediction test is claimed. The study addresses fixed-gap transport and does not include a free surface, wetting, trapped gas, or a moving template. [draft:JC04]

## 结论段英文候选

Within the sampled ideal rectangular-groove geometries, the arithmetic–harmonic local cubic-law closure overestimates both principal depth-integrated transport coefficients. The unequal discrepancies affect the anisotropy and the predicted direction of oblique flux as well as its magnitude. The adopted empirical correction improves the reported sample residuals, but retains gap dependence and a larger transverse discrepancy at the smallest examined gap. Geometry-specific refinement helps qualify these comparisons without establishing a full-domain numerical uncertainty bound. The correction should therefore be reported as a data-informed relation evaluated on available samples. Further predictive use requires a separately defined test and a numerical reference adequate for its claimed accuracy. [draft:JC05]
